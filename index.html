      About SAT Word Study

      A vocabulary app for the SAT: flashcards, quizzes in the SAT's Words in Context format, and a class leaderboard.

      How studying works

      1. Flashcards. Study a set of 10 words. Tap Show meaning (or the card) to see each word's meaning, usage, and an example, then go on to the next card.
      2. Quiz. After each set, take a quiz on those 10 words. You need every answer right to move on. If you miss one, go through the cards again or retake the quiz; you'll get new questions each time.
      3. Learned. A word moves to Learned after you answer it correctly 3 times in a row.
      4. Review quiz. Every 50 words you study, a review quiz on all 50 comes up on its own, plus up to 5 words you learned earlier. Again you need every answer right to go on to the next set. Missing a learned word moves it back to Practice so you can study it again.

      Your practice list holds up to 1,200 words at a time; new words join it as you learn. Finishing a set each day keeps your study streak going.

      Where the words come from

      Words come from the vocabulary questions in College Board's official SAT and PSAT practice tests and ACT's official practice tests, the New Academic Word List (Browne, Culligan & Phillips, CC BY-SA 4.0), Coxhead's Academic Word List, and a list of common SAT words. Definitions, example passages, and quiz answers were written with the help of Claude, an AI model.

      For words your teacher adds, a starting definition comes from the Free Dictionary API (from Wiktionary, CC BY-SA), Claude writes the rest, and your teacher reviews it before it's added.

      What's saved

      When you sign in, the app saves your name and email, your user name and picture, your quiz progress, and when and on what device and browser you visit. Your teacher can see all of it. Other students see only your user name, picture, study streak, and number of words learned, on the leaderboard.

      Reminder emails

      If you haven't studied in more than 2 days, the app emails you a reminder at 8 pm Eastern each evening until you're back. To stop them, ask your teacher.

      Create your profile

      Your user name and picture show on the leaderboard, where other students can see them. Pick something you're happy for your class to see, and don't use your full name.
